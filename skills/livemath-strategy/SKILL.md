### Evaluating relative strength of theorem statements
When a question asks for the *strongest statement that can be proved* (or when options otherwise compete on strength), use the following checks:

1. **Beware of overclaiming.** Do not upgrade "embedding" to "isomorphism onto a leaf," a partial classification to a complete classification, or an implication to a full equivalence, unless the source construction explicitly justifies the stronger form.

2. **Treat the "stronger result" meta-option seriously.** If one choice says "One of the remaining options is correct, but a stronger result can be proven," first identify the strongest specific option that is actually true. Then ask whether the literature proves something *strictly stronger* than that option. Choose the meta-option only if a strictly stronger result is known; otherwise choose the strongest correct specific option.

3. **Pre-finalization check.** Before boxing the answer, explicitly state: (a) the strongest true specific option, and (b) whether any known result exceeds it. If you cannot identify the stronger result concretely, do not default to the meta-option solely out of caution.
